# evidence-synthesis

[Health Research Assistant](README.md) / [Health & Sport domain](../../README.md)

## Evidence Goal

Combine health evidence while preserving heterogeneity, bias and uncertainty.

## Use This Protocol When

Several health studies or guidelines must be combined into a calibrated conclusion.

## Clinical Boundary

Does not turn pooled or majority evidence into a personal medical conclusion.

## Required Evidence Context

Focused question, extracted source records, populations/interventions/outcomes, effect estimates/uncertainty, study quality, guideline context and intended use.

## Evidence Workflow

1. Group genuinely comparable evidence and identify duplicated datasets, shared biases and differences in population, dose, comparator, outcome and follow-up.
2. Weigh directness, design, bias, precision and consistency; synthesize quantitatively only when measures and assumptions permit.
3. Explain heterogeneity and clinically/practically meaningful magnitude, not statistical significance alone; preserve harms and uncertainty.
4. State the general evidence conclusion, confidence, applicability boundary and what an individual decision still requires from a professional.

## Conclusion Calibration

Use categories such as supported, probably supported, uncertain, mixed, probably unsupported or contradicted, tied to evidence quality—not paper counts. Separate absence of evidence from evidence of no meaningful effect.

## Evidence Decision Rules

- Do not translate population averages into promised personal outcomes.
- Changing medical guidance must be checked against current authoritative recommendations.

## Evidence Output

Synthesis with evidence matrix, quality/directness, magnitude/uncertainty, heterogeneity, calibrated conclusion, applicability and remaining decision needs.

## Health Evidence Gates

- Every conclusion traces to included real sources.
- Benefit, harm and uncertainty are presented with compatible denominators and horizons where possible.

## Health Evidence Failure Modes

- **Vote-counting significant studies:** evaluate effects/precision.
- **Harms omitted because benefit studies dominate:** search and report them.

## Handoffs

Guideline Review adds practice context; Risk Assessment frames consequences; clinicians personalize decisions.

# training-volume

[Fitness Coach](README.md) / [Health & Sport domain](../../README.md)

## Coaching Goal

Allocate recoverable training work across movements, muscles and sessions.

## Activate When

A program needs a recoverable amount and distribution of training work.

## Training Boundary

Volume is an exposure to manage, not a universal set target or the sole driver of results.

## Training Context

Goal, exercise/movement categories, current and recent volume, frequency, intensity/effort, experience, performance/recovery trends, time and restrictions.

## Coaching Protocol

1. Choose a meaningful volume unit for the activity and establish the user’s recent tolerated baseline rather than a generic optimum.
2. Allocate work across priority movements/muscles/energy systems and sessions, accounting for overlap, exercise cost and intensity.
3. Increase, maintain or reduce volume from performance, technique, soreness/fatigue and adherence trends; use small changes and protect recovery.
4. Set weekly and session caps or ranges, a deload response and a review window; adapt distribution when schedule compresses.

## Effective and Recoverable Work

Count work that meaningfully serves the target and is performed at intended quality. More volume can add stimulus, fatigue or junk work; the response and opportunity cost determine which.

## Training Decision Rules

- Do not infer equivalence between sets, minutes or distance across very different intensity and movement demands.
- When time falls, preserve priority work and remove low-value overlap first.

## Program Output

Volume plan with unit/baseline, distribution, overlap notes, progression/reduction rule, session cap, tracking and review.

## Training Quality Gates

- Volume, intensity and frequency form a recoverable whole.
- The plan can be reduced during a busy week without compressing missed work.

## Training Failure Modes

- **Generic set target ignores history:** anchor to baseline.
- **Indirect work double-counted:** state overlap assumptions.

## Handoffs

Intensity sets effort; Program Design integrates; Recovery Adjustment responds to fatigue; Progress Review evaluates trend.

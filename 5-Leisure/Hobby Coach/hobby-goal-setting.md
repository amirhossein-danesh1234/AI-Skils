# hobby-goal-setting

[Hobby Coach](README.md) / [Leisure domain](../../README.md)

## Hobby Goal

Define a hobby direction that preserves enjoyment while enabling growth or completion.

## Activate When

A chosen or potential hobby needs a direction that balances enjoyment, learning and commitment.

## Hobby Boundary

A hobby need not become productive, monetized or measurable.

## Practice or Project Context

Hobby/possible hobbies, reasons, desired experience/output/social element, current level, time/budget/tools, safety constraints and disliked pressure.

## Coaching Workflow

1. Clarify whether the user wants exploration, relaxation, expression, community, mastery or a project; allow several motives without forcing metrics.
2. Choose a short horizon and a minimum experiment or observable capability that would make the hobby worthwhile.
3. Bound time, money and equipment, define non-goals and avoid turning the hobby into work or monetization unless requested.
4. Set a review that can deepen, change direction, pause or stop based on enjoyment and learning evidence.

## Hobby Worthiness

A hobby goal should increase access to the desired experience. Measurement is optional; curiosity, regular enjoyment or completing a personally meaningful project can be valid outcomes.

## Hobby Decision Rules

- Do not adopt expert norms as the user’s goal.
- Exploration may end with “not for me” and still succeed.

## Hobby Output

Hobby goal with motive, short outcome/experiment, constraints, non-goals, minimum version, review and stop/change options.

## Learning and Enjoyment Gates

- The goal fits free-time capacity and preserves enjoyment.
- No purchase or public commitment is assumed.

## Hobby Failure Modes

- **Productivity framing takes over:** return to motive.
- **Ambitious identity before trial:** stage exploration.

## Handoffs

Practice Planning implements; Project Ideas supply experiment; Personal Strategist handles larger identity trade-offs.

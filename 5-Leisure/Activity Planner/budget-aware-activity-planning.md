# budget-aware-activity-planning

[Activity Planner](README.md) / [Leisure domain](../../README.md)

## Activity Goal

Choose leisure options within a clear total spending limit.

## Activate When

An outing or leisure choice must fit an explicit spending ceiling.

## Coordination Boundary

Does not spend or optimize cost while ignoring access, travel and enjoyment.

## Activity Context

Total/group/per-person budget and currency, who pays, location/time, participants, interests, transport, food/equipment needs, cancellation exposure and optionality.

## Planning Workflow

1. Define the budget boundary and whether it covers transport, food, fees, rentals, tips/taxes and contingency.
2. Build total-cost ranges for a few suitable options using current prices when material, separating known price, estimate and optional spend.
3. Compare enjoyment fit, hidden costs, cancellation/refund and lower-cost substitutes; do not treat free as automatically accessible or desirable.
4. Select a base plan within the ceiling plus a cheaper fallback, leaving any purchase or reservation for explicit approval.

## All-In Leisure Cost

Use door-to-door and participation cost, not ticket price: transport, parking, food, equipment, booking fees and cancellation risk may dominate. Show per-person responsibility for groups.

## Activity Decision Rules

- Do not consume the entire budget when uncertainty is high.
- Material prices and terms require current verification.

## Activity Output

Budgeted activity plan with cost table/labels, payment assumptions, fit comparison, contingency, fallback and approval gate.

## Feasibility Gates

- The upper credible base cost fits the stated ceiling.
- No optional cost is silently treated as required or vice versa.

## Activity Failure Modes

- **Cheap option ignores transport/access:** total it.
- **Budget becomes pressure to spend maximum:** preserve savings.

## Handoffs

Group Activity resolves shared payment; Personal Finance checks affordability for material spend; Travel Budgeting owns trips.

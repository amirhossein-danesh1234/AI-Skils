# Retrospective

## Purpose

Turn team experience into a small, testable improvement.

## When to Use

A team reviews a delivery period or recurring friction.

## When Not to Use

Do not use the session for public blame or confidential performance management.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Recent events, outcomes, team observations, prior actions, and psychological-safety constraints.

### Helpful

Team working agreements, goals, backlog readiness, capacity, delivery observations, and impediment evidence.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Agreed improvement experiment with problem, evidence, owner, expected effect, and review date.

## Operating Principles

Use Scrum commitments when the team actually uses Scrum; otherwise adapt flow practices openly. Do not present a hybrid process as strict Scrum.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect prior actions and whether they changed behavior.
2. Collect observations and distinguish events from interpretations.
3. Explore systemic causes and choose a problem within useful influence.
4. Agree a small experiment and define what observation will justify keeping or changing it.

## Decision Rules

- If a concern is a confidential people issue, route it privately rather than debating it publicly.
- If too many actions compete, select the most useful feasible experiment.

## Validation

- Is the action concrete and within authority?
- Will the next review assess effect rather than only completion?

## Common Failure Modes

- Same complaints without experiments: assign a test.
- Forced positivity suppresses evidence: allow respectful disagreement.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Team Manager handles people issues; Operations handles cross-team process; Project Manager handles external constraints.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

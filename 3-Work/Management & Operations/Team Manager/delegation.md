# Delegation

## Purpose

Transfer a bounded outcome with sufficient context, authority, and support.

## When to Use

Work should move to another person without losing accountability.

## When Not to Use

Do not delegate decisions the recipient lacks authority or capacity to make.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Outcome, recipient capability, authority limits, deadline, constraints, and acceptance.

### Helpful

Role expectations, observed work, capacity, skills, commitments, organizational policy, and relevant employee context with privacy safeguards.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Delegation agreement with outcome, boundaries, resources, checkpoints, escalation, and definition of done.

## Operating Principles

Use observable evidence, consistent criteria, and private respectful feedback; do not equate busyness, meeting attendance, or story points with performance.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the result and why it matters before prescribing steps.
2. Assess readiness and choose an appropriate level of autonomy and support.
3. Agree authority, constraints, available resources, and review points.
4. Verify shared understanding and review outcomes without unnecessary micromanagement.

## Decision Rules

- If the recipient lacks a required skill, provide support or narrow the assignment.
- If a decision crosses the mandate, require escalation rather than implicit permission.

## Validation

- Can the recipient explain success and limits in their own terms?
- Are workload and dependencies feasible?

## Common Failure Modes

- Task dumped without authority: supply decision rights.
- Delegation becomes surveillance: use proportionate checkpoints.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Project Manager aligns delivery; relevant specialists provide support; role-definition.md clarifies mandate.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

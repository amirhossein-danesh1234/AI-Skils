# Sales Process

Context: [Sales Strategist](README.md).

## Purpose

Define a repeatable buying-support process with clear evidence gates.

## Activate When

Sales work is inconsistent or handoffs lose customer context.

## Do Not Use When

Do not force every buyer through the same steps regardless of complexity.

## Required Context

**Needed:** Real won/lost buying journeys, deal types, current owners, and approval/handoff needs.

**Can be deferred or bounded:** A simple low-risk deal may skip steps that add no evidence or control.

## Workflow

1. Inspect actual won and lost journeys rather than idealized internal steps.
2. Define stages around buyer progress and verified commitments.
3. Assign ownership for discovery, proposal, negotiation, approval, and fulfillment handoff.
4. Test the process on different deal sizes and remove steps without value.

## Stage Exit Evidence

Define stage transitions by buyer progress: problem agreed, decision process understood, offer evaluated, conditions accepted. Test a small and a complex deal for unnecessary ceremony. Transfer promises and dependencies into fulfillment so the customer does not have to renegotiate expectations after purchase.

## Decision Rules

- If a stage advances only because a seller sent something, require buyer evidence where meaningful.
- If low-risk deals need fewer steps, define a lightweight path.

## Output Contract

Sales process with stages, entry/exit evidence, owners, customer actions, exceptions, and handoffs.

## Quality Gates

- Can two sellers classify the same opportunity consistently?
- Are commitments and delivery expectations transferred accurately?
- Two sellers can classify the same opportunity consistently using observable evidence.

## Failure Modes

- Process serves CRM rather than buyer: align to buying progress.
- Handoff loses promises: record scope and terms.

## Handoffs

Operations owns fulfillment; Product Manager verifies promises; Team Manager clarifies roles.

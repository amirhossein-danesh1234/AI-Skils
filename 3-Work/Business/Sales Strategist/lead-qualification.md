# Lead Qualification

## Purpose

Decide whether a prospect merits pursuit and what evidence is missing.

## When to Use

A new lead or stalled opportunity needs prioritization.

## When Not to Use

Do not use a framework as a rigid interrogation or assume every lead should be converted.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Customer problem, fit, urgency, buying process, authority, budget context, and delivery constraints.

### Helpful

Customer context, offer, qualification evidence, stage definitions, pricing authority, pipeline data, and delivery constraints.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Qualification assessment with fit, evidence, gaps, disqualifiers, and next buyer action.

## Operating Principles

Optimize mutual fit, evidence-based stage progression, and sustainable deal economics rather than activity volume.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the prospect’s actual problem and current workaround.
2. Assess value, urgency, access to decision makers, purchase process, and economic fit.
3. Identify technical, commercial, and delivery constraints.
4. Choose pursue, nurture, clarify, or disqualify with reasons.

## Decision Rules

- If the product cannot solve the essential problem, disqualify or narrow the offer rather than promise future capability.
- If buying authority is unclear, seek the process instead of assuming the contact can approve.

## Validation

- Are qualification claims supported by customer evidence?
- Does the next action resolve a meaningful uncertainty?

## Common Failure Modes

- Budget alone defines fit: assess need and process.
- Optimism advances stages: require observable evidence.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Product Manager confirms capability; Financial Analyst checks deal economics; Sales process defines stage rules.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

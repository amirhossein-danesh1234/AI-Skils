# Business Model

Context: [Business Strategist](README.md).

## Purpose

Define how a business creates, delivers, and captures value.

## Activate When

An offering lacks a credible economic and operating model.

## Do Not Use When

Financial Analyst validates calculations; do not treat a canvas as proof of demand.

## Required Context

**Needed:** Customer/payer, offer, delivery mechanism, and resource constraints.

**Can be deferred or bounded:** Unknown revenue or cost drivers remain hypotheses; a business canvas is not evidence.

## Workflow

1. Inspect who benefits, who pays, and how value is delivered today.
2. Map acquisition, fulfillment, support, partners, and cash timing.
3. Compare revenue models against buying behavior and incremental costs.
4. Identify the assumptions most likely to invalidate viability and design cheap tests.

## Value and Cash Loop

Trace one customer from acquisition to delivered value, payment, support, and renewal/exit. Identify who captures margin and who finances the delay. Test a failed delivery or return; a viable model must explain these economics as well as the ideal sale.

## Decision Rules

- If beneficiary and payer differ, validate both incentives.
- If revenue scales but losses or working-capital needs grow faster, redesign before scaling.

## Output Contract

Business-model hypothesis with value flows, revenue mechanism, cost drivers, risks, and validation priorities.

## Quality Gates

- Are value, revenue, costs, and cash timing internally consistent?
- Does each key assumption have evidence or a test?
- The unit, payer, value delivered, and incremental costs refer to the same economic activity.

## Failure Modes

- Business-model diagram replaces economics: quantify drivers.
- Partner dependency assumed free: include terms and leverage.

## Handoffs

Financial Analyst tests economics; Market Researcher validates demand; Operations Manager assesses delivery feasibility.

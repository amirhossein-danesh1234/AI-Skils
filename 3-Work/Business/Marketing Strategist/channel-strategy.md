# Channel Strategy

Context: [Marketing Strategist](README.md).

## Purpose

Select and sequence marketing channels based on audience behavior and economics.

## Activate When

A team must choose where to invest limited marketing capacity.

## Do Not Use When

Do not recommend every popular channel or infer fit from competitor presence alone.

## Required Context

**Needed:** Buyer journey, reachable audience, budget/capability constraints, and available channel evidence.

**Can be deferred or bounded:** New channels can begin as small hypotheses; competitor presence alone does not prove fit.

## Workflow

1. Inspect how target buyers seek information and make purchases.
2. Evaluate channel intent, reach, saturation, economics, control, and time horizon.
3. Choose a small set with distinct roles rather than duplicated activity.
4. Define experiments, measurement limits, and conditions to scale or stop.

## Portfolio Role

Assign each channel a specific job such as demand creation, high-intent capture, education, or retention. Account for audience overlap, learning delay, dependence on platforms, and content/sales capacity. Choose a few testable routes rather than spreading effort across every fashionable channel.

## Decision Rules

- If a channel requires capabilities the team lacks, include their cost or choose another route.
- If learning takes longer than the cash horizon, reduce commitment or reject it.

## Output Contract

Channel portfolio with role, hypothesis, cost, learning cadence, dependencies, and exit criteria.

## Quality Gates

- Does each channel have a distinct role and measurable decision?
- Are interactions and attribution limits acknowledged?
- Each channel has a distinct role, feasible operating cost, and decision threshold.

## Failure Modes

- Trend-driven channel choice: use audience evidence.
- Diversification spreads effort too thin: bound the portfolio.

## Handoffs

Sales evaluates buyer access; Financial Analyst checks cost; Marketing analysis reviews outcomes.

# Pricing Research

## Purpose

Collect evidence about price levels, willingness to pay, and buying trade-offs.

## When to Use

A pricing decision lacks a defensible external evidence base.

## When Not to Use

Commercial-pricing.md sets deal economics; research does not determine price alone.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Offer, segment, comparable alternatives, pricing unit, geography, and research method.

### Helpful

Decision question, geography, customer definition, product category, time horizon, source access, and research budget.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Pricing evidence with normalized comparisons, behavioral signals, study limits, and test recommendations.

## Operating Principles

Distinguish primary observations, secondary analysis, company claims, analyst estimates, community reports, inference, and unknowns; verify freshness.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect list prices, discounts, contract terms, bundles, and total customer cost.
2. Normalize pricing units and compare equivalent customer situations.
3. Use appropriate direct research or experiments while distinguishing stated willingness from actual purchase.
4. Identify reference prices, value drivers, and adoption barriers with uncertainty.

## Decision Rules

- If competitors bundle different scope, do not compare headline price directly.
- If evidence is hypothetical, validate with an ethical real decision before broad pricing changes.

## Validation

- Are currency, tax, period, unit, and contract conditions aligned?
- Are sample and method limitations explicit?

## Common Failure Modes

- Survey answer treated as demand curve: validate behavior.
- List price assumed realized price: note discount uncertainty.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Financial Analyst models margin; Marketing tests framing; Sales supplies objections and realized terms.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

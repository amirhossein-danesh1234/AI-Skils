# Source Validation

Context: [Market Researcher](README.md).

## Purpose

Assess whether a source can support a specific market claim.

## Activate When

A consequential claim relies on uncertain provenance or quality.

## Do Not Use When

Do not equate prestige, recency, or multiple citations with correctness.

## Required Context

**Needed:** Exact claim, original source, method/definitions, and decision consequence.

**Can be deferred or bounded:** Unavailable methodology reduces the supported claim; it does not require discarding all source use.

## Workflow

1. Trace the claim to its original evidence rather than a repeated summary.
2. Inspect sample, collection method, definitions, geography, period, and conflicts of interest.
3. Check freshness and whether subsequent events change applicability.
4. Compare independent evidence and classify the claim as supported, bounded, disputed, or unknown.

## Evidence Chain

Trace repeated numbers to their origin, separating article publication from underlying observation date. Check who funded or benefits from the claim, population coverage, and whether a newer event invalidates applicability. Carry qualifications into the downstream claim rather than leaving them only in footnotes.

## Decision Rules

- If methodology is unavailable, lower confidence and avoid precise extrapolation.
- If sources share an origin, do not treat repetition as independent corroboration.

## Output Contract

Source appraisal with provenance, method, incentives, freshness, corroboration, and usable claim limits.

## Quality Gates

- Does the source actually support the wording and scope of the claim?
- Are limitations carried into downstream summaries?
- The cited source supports the claim’s actual scope, units, and strength.

## Failure Modes

- Citation laundering hides weak origin: trace provenance.
- Recent article repeats old data: distinguish publication and data dates.

## Handoffs

[Market-research.md](market-research.md) consumes validated evidence; strategy owners decide whether uncertainty requires more research.

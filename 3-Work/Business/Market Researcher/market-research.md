# Market Research

Context: [Market Researcher](README.md).

## Purpose

Gather and synthesize evidence sufficient for a defined market decision.

## Activate When

A market question lacks reliable customer, demand, or industry evidence.

## Do Not Use When

Do not browse indefinitely or substitute desk research for direct customer validation when behavior is decisive.

## Required Context

**Needed:** Decision question, market boundaries, and evidence required to choose.

**Can be deferred or bounded:** A research budget can be small; access limits must be reported rather than filled with plausible numbers.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision and claims that must be supported or falsified.
2. Choose primary and secondary methods with explicit inclusion criteria and freshness needs.
3. Collect evidence with provenance, date, definitions, and incentives of the source.
4. Triangulate independent sources and preserve disagreement rather than averaging incompatible claims.
5. Stop when the decision has adequate evidence or the remaining gap requires a different method.

## Evidence Register and Stopping Rule

Record each material claim with its source, original evidence date, publication date, geography, population, method, incentive or conflict, and the exact claim it supports. Classify evidence as primary observation, secondary analysis, company claim, analyst estimate, community report, inference, or unknown. A source can be useful without supporting every claim attributed to it.

Choose methods around the decision: desk research for market structure and public facts; interviews for context and mechanisms; surveys for carefully bounded estimates with sampling limits; observed behavior or transactions for stronger evidence of action. Do not imply that a convenience sample estimates an entire market.

Search for contrary evidence and trace repeated claims to their origin. Keep incompatible definitions separate until reconciled. If access is limited, state what could not be checked rather than filling gaps with plausible numbers.

Stop when the decision’s important claims are supported to a risk-appropriate level, further search has low marginal value, or the remaining uncertainty requires a different method. End with a decision-ready synthesis and the next evidence action, not an unfiltered source dump.

## Claim Coverage

Build a question-to-claim-to-evidence map before browsing. Track independent evidence chains and stop collecting repeated summaries of the same report. Seek disconfirming evidence for the favored option; if the remaining uncertainty is buying behavior, switch method instead of endlessly expanding desk research.

## Decision Rules

- If sources repeat one original claim, count them as one evidence chain.
- If buying behavior matters, distinguish stated interest from observed purchase or commitment.

## Output Contract

Research brief with method, source register, findings, contradictions, limitations, and next validation.

## Quality Gates

- Can important claims be traced to consulted sources?
- Are coverage gaps and evidence strength explicit?
- The synthesis states what the evidence permits deciding and what needs another method.

## Failure Modes

- Search volume mistaken for demand: inspect buying context.
- Only confirming sources collected: actively seek contrary evidence.

## Handoffs

Business and Product Strategists interpret choices; Financial Analyst models consequences; user research may require consent and qualified methods.

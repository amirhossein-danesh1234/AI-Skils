# Commercial Risk Analysis

## Purpose

Assess deal-specific exposure across supplier, payment, delivery, quality, and compliance.

## When to Use

A trade deal or commercial commitment needs a risk decision.

## When Not to Use

Do not reduce risk to supplier price or a single probability score.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Deal terms, counterparties, route, goods, payment schedule, quality requirements, and exposure limits.

### Helpful

Product specifications, quantities, route, jurisdictions, supplier evidence, quote currency and validity, payment terms, and delivery-term version.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Deal risk register with loss scenarios, controls, cash exposure, triggers, residual risk, and approval conditions.

## Operating Principles

Reconcile price, freight, insurance, duties, taxes, lead times, quality, and working capital. Delivery terms do not automatically settle title or payment.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Trace cash and goods through the full transaction and identify points of irreversible exposure.
2. Assess counterparty failure, quality dispute, delay, FX, documentation, logistics, and compliance scenarios.
3. Compare controls such as staged payment, inspection, insurance, alternate supply, and narrower scope.
4. Test correlated failures and identify who may accept remaining exposure.

## Decision Rules

- If maximum loss exceeds the authorized limit, restructure or escalate before commitment.
- If insurance is relied on, verify coverage, exclusions, and claims requirements.

## Validation

- Do controls address the actual loss mechanism?
- Are cash timing and recovery assumptions realistic?

## Common Failure Modes

- Risk transferred on paper but not enforceable: verify terms.
- Independent-risk scoring misses combined delay and FX loss: test scenarios.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Financial Analyst quantifies exposure; legal and insurance specialists confirm terms; executive owner approves material risk.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

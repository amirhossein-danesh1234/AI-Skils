# Incoterms Analysis

## Purpose

Clarify delivery obligations, cost allocation, and risk transfer under the chosen trade rule.

## When to Use

A goods contract needs a suitable delivery term or conflicting interpretations must be resolved.

## When Not to Use

Incoterms do not by themselves determine title, payment, contract validity, or all legal obligations.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Goods, transport mode, route, named place or port, rule/version, parties’ capabilities, and contract terms.

### Helpful

Product specifications, quantities, route, jurisdictions, supplier evidence, quote currency and validity, payment terms, and delivery-term version.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Term comparison with delivery/risk point, cost responsibilities, transport/insurance/customs duties, and unresolved legal questions.

## Operating Principles

Reconcile price, freight, insurance, duties, taxes, lead times, quality, and working capital. Delivery terms do not automatically settle title or payment.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the exact rule, version, and named location in the proposed documents.
2. Map delivery, risk, carriage, insurance, export/import clearance, and practical control of each step.
3. Compare candidate rules against transport mode, local legal constraints, and actual party capabilities.
4. Reconcile the chosen rule with payment, title, inspection, remedies, and contract-specific variations.

## Responsibility Matrix

For the proposed transaction, compare each party’s responsibility for packaging, export clearance, pickup, main carriage, transit, insurance, import clearance, final delivery, unloading, and required transport documents as applicable. Verify details against the selected ICC rule/version and the exact named place; do not fill a universal matrix from memory.

Separate the point where risk transfers from who pays a cost. Review any contract variation explicitly, because changing a cost allocation does not necessarily change delivery or risk transfer. Confirm that the parties can perform assigned customs and transport obligations under applicable local rules.

Review title transfer, payment, inspection, nonconforming goods, remedies, governing law, and dispute terms separately with qualified support. These are not settled merely by writing an Incoterms abbreviation. Reconcile the commercial quotation, contract, shipping instructions, and payment documents before commitment.

Primary reference: [ICC Incoterms questions and answers](https://library.iccwbo.org/clp/clp-incoterms-qa-2020.htm). Obtain current jurisdiction-specific advice when execution depends on legal or customs interpretation.

## Decision Rules

- If the named place is vague, clarify it before accepting the term.
- If a party cannot lawfully or practically perform its assigned clearance duty, choose another arrangement with qualified advice.

## Validation

- Are cost and risk transfer distinguished rather than assumed simultaneous?
- Do documents use one consistent rule/version and precise place?

## Common Failure Modes

- Three-letter code treated as complete contract: review other terms.
- Transport-mode mismatch: verify rule applicability with current ICC guidance.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Logistics providers verify execution; legal and customs specialists confirm jurisdiction constraints; Financial Analyst checks cash implications.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

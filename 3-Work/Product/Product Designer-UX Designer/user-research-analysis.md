# User Research Analysis

## Purpose

Translate user research into supported design implications.

## When to Use

Interviews, observations, or usability sessions need synthesis.

## When Not to Use

Do not infer prevalence from a convenience sample or replace research with invented personas.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Research question, raw notes, participant context, method, consent limits, and product scenario.

### Helpful

User tasks, research evidence, current screens and flows, constraints, accessibility needs, and business rules.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Findings linked to observations, contradictory cases, confidence, design implications, and follow-up questions.

## Operating Principles

Separate observed behavior from interpretation. Optimize comprehension and task completion, not screen count or visual novelty.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect sampling, prompts, recording quality, and missing user groups.
2. Separate direct observations and quotations from researcher interpretation.
3. Cluster recurring needs and behaviors while retaining contradictory cases.
4. Connect each finding to a design decision and identify the next evidence that could change it.

## When Raw Research Is Missing

Return the decision question, the participant or evidence needs, and a proportionate collection plan with consent and authorization requirements. Assign a proposed research owner and state which design decision the evidence must inform. Existing support examples can be inspected within their permitted use, but they are not automatically representative user research. Mark synthesis blocked until observations exist; do not fabricate findings, quotations, prevalence, or user emotions. A useful research handoff can be complete even when the requested synthesis is not.

## Decision Rules

- If the method cannot support a frequency claim, report qualitative patterns without percentages.
- If one vocal participant dominates, test whether the finding appears elsewhere.

## Validation

- Can each major finding be traced to raw evidence?
- Are selection bias and alternative explanations visible?

## Common Failure Modes

- Theme counts mistaken for population truth: state sampling limits.
- Recommendations detached from observations: show the evidence chain.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Product Analyst triangulates behavioral data; Product Manager decides scope; Researcher on Core is a future shared-method dependency, not an authored protocol today.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

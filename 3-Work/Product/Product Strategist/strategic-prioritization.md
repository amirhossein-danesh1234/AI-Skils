# Strategic Prioritization

## Purpose

Rank product-level bets under scarce capacity and strategic uncertainty.

## When to Use

Leadership must choose which outcomes or opportunities receive investment.

## When Not to Use

Product Manager prioritizes deliverable backlog items; this skill allocates attention across strategic bets.

## Required Inputs

### Required

Candidate bets, strategy, opportunity costs, capacity, evidence, risk, and decision authority.

### Helpful

Customer evidence, company constraints, alternatives, current product performance, market context, and strategic horizon.

### Optional

Previous decisions, comparable cases, or preferred output format. Their absence must not block a bounded first pass.

If a required fact is missing, identify which decision it affects. Continue with a labeled assumption only when the consequence is low risk and reversible; otherwise ask the smallest question needed. Do not invent project facts, approvals, measurements, or test results.

## Output

Ordered investment choices, rejected or delayed bets, rationale, sensitivity, and review triggers.

## Operating Principles

Vision describes the desired future; strategy chooses a path; roadmap sequences outcome bets. Evidence of demand is not proof of durable product-market fit.

Separate verified facts, supplied information, external evidence, assumptions, estimates, inferences, opinions, and unknowns. Show the basis of consequential claims. Scale rigor to team capacity and risk; a framework is optional, and its limitations must be stated when used.

## Workflow

1. Inspect whether each bet addresses the chosen customer and strategic obstacle.
2. Remove infeasible or policy-violating options before scoring preference.
3. Compare expected customer value, advantage, learning, economics, dependencies, and downside.
4. Test whether plausible changes in assumptions reverse the order; choose a portfolio the team can actually support.

## Decision Rules

- If scores are too sensitive to speculative estimates, use a learning experiment or qualitative tiering.
- If a bet consumes the same scarce resource as a higher-value one, make displacement explicit.

## Validation

- Does the selected set fit real constraints and avoid double-counted benefits?
- Are urgent obligations separated from elective strategic bets?

## Common Failure Modes

- Scoring ritual hides judgment: explain criteria and uncertainty.
- Everything marked critical: identify what will not receive resources.

## Escalation and Collaboration

Business Strategist and Financial Analyst evaluate company trade-offs; Product Manager and Project Manager assess execution consequences.

Do not perform external writes, production changes, purchases, disclosures, or commitments merely because this protocol recommends them. Confirm the actual task authorizes the action and stop at the boundary of that authority.

## Completion Criteria

The defined output answers the activation question; its decision rules and validation checks have been applied. Explicitly separate a proposal from an approved decision and a planned test from an observed result. Record the recommendation or changed artifact, the validation actually performed, remaining uncertainty, and the next action with an owner or proposed owner. Include priority, dependency, and definition of done when work remains. A blocked execution can produce a useful assessment, but it is not a completed implementation.

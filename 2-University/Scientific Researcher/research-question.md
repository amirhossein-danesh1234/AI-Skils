# research-question

[Scientific Researcher](README.md) / [University domain](../../README.md)

## Research Aim

Turn a broad topic into a focused, answerable and worthwhile research question.

## Use This Protocol When

A broad topic or practical concern needs a focused scientific question and possible hypothesis.

## Evidence Boundary

Do not lock a method or expected result into the question without justification.

## Required Research Record

Topic/problem, intended contribution/decision, prior evidence, target population/system, feasible data/methods, constraints, ethics and uncertainty.

## Research Protocol

1. Map the phenomenon and distinguish descriptive, comparative, causal, predictive, mechanistic or interpretive aims.
2. Specify population/system, exposure/intervention, comparator where relevant, outcome/construct, context and time horizon without prematurely fixing method.
3. Check answerability, observability, prior-work overlap, scope and consequence; define key terms and the inference the answer should support.
4. Draft the primary question and optional directional/non-directional hypothesis only when theory/evidence warrants it; list exclusions and feasibility unknowns.

## Question Quality Test

A strong question is clear, bounded, important, answerable and linked to evidence that could change belief. A hypothesis is a testable claim, not a desired result; exploratory work may be better without a directional hypothesis.

## Evidence Rules

- Do not smuggle causal language into an observationally answerable question.
- Separate research objective from stakeholder preference or expected outcome.

## Research Output

Question brief with aim type, defined elements, rationale/prior evidence, primary question, hypothesis status, scope exclusions, feasibility/ethics and next evidence need.

## Provenance and Quality Gates

- The question can map to observable data and a defensible analysis.
- Terms and implied causal/temporal direction are unambiguous.

## Research Failure Modes

- **Question is only a topic label:** specify relationship/output.
- **Method named before inference is clear:** return to the target claim.

## Handoffs

Literature Review tests prior work; Methodology Design operationalizes; Academic Planner handles thesis milestones.

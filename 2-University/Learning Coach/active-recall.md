# active-recall

[Learning Coach](README.md) / [University domain](../../README.md)

## Learning Aim

Design retrieval prompts that reveal what the learner can produce without cues.

## Activate When

The learner needs retrieval practice for concepts, procedures, derivations or relationships.

## Coaching Boundary

Re-reading and recognition are not active recall; subject correctness remains tutor-owned.

## Learner Evidence

Learning objectives, prerequisite level, source material, representative assessment demands, current unaided performance and feedback source.

## Coaching Loop

1. Translate each objective into something the learner must produce, explain, derive, discriminate or apply without looking.
2. Create prompts with appropriate cue strength and varied direction. Include explanation and application, not only isolated fact cards.
3. Run retrieval before review, record confidence and answer, then compare with an authoritative solution and classify the error.
4. Schedule the next attempt by demonstrated difficulty and require transfer to a new example before declaring mastery.

## Retrieval Ladder

Progress from free recall to structured explanation, derivation/application and mixed transfer. A correct guess or familiar-looking answer is weaker evidence than a reproducible explanation with checks.

## Durable Learning Rules

- Hints should be delayed and graduated; reveal only enough to restart reasoning.
- Retire or reduce prompts that are consistently easy and low-value.

## Learning Plan Output

Recall set with objective mapping, prompts, delayed answers/checks, scoring rule, error log and next-review criteria.

## Mastery Checks

- Prompts sample the actual knowledge and reasoning required.
- Feedback corrects misconceptions rather than rewarding confidence or exposure.

## Coaching Failure Modes

- **Turning notes into hundreds of trivia cards:** target decisions, relations and failure points.
- **Reviewing before attempting recall:** preserve the diagnostic value.

## Handoffs

Subject tutors validate content; Practice Design builds extended tasks; Revision Planning schedules revisits.

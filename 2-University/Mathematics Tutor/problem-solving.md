# problem-solving

[Mathematics Tutor](README.md) / [University domain](../../README.md)

## Mathematical Goal

Solve a mathematical problem by classifying structure and justifying each step.

## Use This For

A mathematical exercise or open calculation needs structured reasoning and a justified result.

## Validity Boundary

Do not apply a technique before checking its hypotheses, domain and requested form.

## Definitions and Preconditions

Exact statement, domain, definitions, permitted results/methods, learner attempt, desired form and proof/precision standard.

## Reasoning Protocol

1. Parse quantifiers, domain, givens and target; test whether the problem is determined and identify relevant structure or invariants.
2. Generate candidate routes from definitions, theorems, transformations, representations or simpler cases; verify hypotheses before selection.
3. Execute symbolically with reasons at nontrivial steps, tracking equivalence versus implication and all case splits.
4. Verify by substitution, boundary/special cases, independent reasoning, bounds or counterexample search; communicate why the route works.

## Strategy Signals

Use structure—linearity, symmetry, monotonicity, conservation, convexity, orthogonality, analyticity or recurrence—to narrow methods. Similar-looking notation alone is not a strategy.

## Validity Rules

- Preserve excluded values and branch conditions throughout.
- If uniqueness is not established, do not report one found solution as all solutions.

## Mathematical Output

Worked reasoning with formal setup, strategy rationale, justified steps, cases/domain, result, verification and a related learner prompt.

## Independent Checks

- Every cited theorem has satisfied hypotheses.
- The final answer addresses all quantifiers and solution branches.

## Logical Failure Modes

- **Pattern matching to a remembered formula:** derive applicability.
- **Checking only the final value:** audit the logic and completeness.

## Handoffs

Concept Explanation fills definitions; Symbolic Derivation handles long transformations; Proof Analysis handles formal arguments.

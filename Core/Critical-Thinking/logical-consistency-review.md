# Logical Consistency Review

Context: [Critical-Thinking](README.md).

## Purpose

Reconcile definitions, quantifiers, constraints and contradictions.

## When to Use

A document, set of requirements or proposed model may contain conflicting statements or impossible constraints.

## Boundary

Consistency is necessary but does not establish empirical truth.

## Inputs

Actual statements with versions, definitions, quantifiers, units, time scopes and intended interpretation.

## Method

1. Normalize vocabulary, units, population and time. Distinguish a genuine contradiction from different conditions, changed versions or different levels of aggregation.
2. Translate material statements into explicit conditions: all/some/none, necessary/sufficient, must/may, before/after. Track whether an exception changes the universal claim.
3. Test boundary cases and a minimal counterexample. For constraints, find a small conflicting subset rather than declaring the entire plan impossible without showing why.
4. Propose the smallest clarification or revision, naming the statement whose meaning would change. Send conflicting preferences or authorities to the actual owner rather than silently choosing.

## Consistency Versus Truth

A contradiction requires incompatible claims about the same subject under the same conditions. “Usually” and “always” have different burdens. Check circular definitions and circular justifications: a conclusion cannot be its own only support. Removing contradictions makes a set coherent, not empirically correct or ethically acceptable.

## Output

Located conflict, normalized conditions, counterexample/minimal inconsistent subset, and proposed resolution with owner approval where meaning changes.

## Quality Checks

- Each reported contradiction survives the scope/version check.
- Repairs preserve intended constraints and do not silently weaken a hard requirement to obtain consistency.

## Handoffs

[Argument-analysis](argument-analysis.md) checks inference; domain owners resolve meanings that depend on professional rules.

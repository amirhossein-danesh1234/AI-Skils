# Evidence Evaluation

Context: [Critical-Thinking](README.md).

## Purpose

Assess how strongly specific observations support a specific claim.

## When to Use

A specific claim needs a judgment of how much its supplied evidence supports it.

## Boundary

Researcher source-evaluation checks the source; this checks the inference.

## Inputs

Precisely scoped claim, observations/studies, how evidence was produced, comparison cases and consequences of error.

## Method

1. Match evidence to the actual claim: existence, prevalence, association, causation, generalization or prediction require different support. Evidence for one does not automatically establish another.
2. Inspect relevance, measurement validity, selection, uncertainty and alternative explanations. Determine whether the observation would also be common if a competing claim were true.
3. Trace independence. Multiple summaries of one experiment, or several metrics from one sample, do not create independent replications. Separate a vendor claim from observed results.
4. State the strongest conclusion licensed by the evidence and the unresolved premise preventing a stronger one. Identify the next observation most likely to discriminate, if the decision warrants it.

## Strength Is Claim-Relative

Credibility of a source is an input, not the answer. A primary report can be weak for causal inference yet useful for implementation details; a review can summarize a body of evidence more reliably than one anecdote. Absence of detected evidence supports absence only when the search/test had an adequate chance of detecting the effect. Use qualitative confidence with reasons unless probabilities can be justified.

## Output

Claim-to-evidence matrix, supported scope, independence/quality limits, competing interpretation and a calibrated conclusion.

## Quality Checks

- Confidence tracks the inferential gap, not the number of citations or the fluency of a source.
- No absolute “no effect” claim is inferred from an underpowered, insensitive or incomplete observation.

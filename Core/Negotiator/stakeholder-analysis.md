# Stakeholder Analysis

Context: [Negotiator](README.md).

## Purpose

Map affected parties, decision rights, vetoes and legitimate consultation.

## When to Use

An agreement or decision affects multiple parties and authority or consultation is unclear.

## Boundary

Influence does not equal consent or authority.

## Inputs

Decision, affected parties, roles, formal/informal authority, dependencies, rights and communication mandate.

## Method

1. Identify decision makers, implementers, directly affected people, gatekeepers and legitimate representatives. Include low-influence parties who bear consequences.
2. Separate influence, formal authority, expertise, consent rights and implementation responsibility. Verify who can approve which term rather than assuming the most senior or vocal person can bind everyone.
3. Map interests, constraints, dependencies and potential conflicts using evidence; mark unknowns. Avoid inferring personal traits or sensitive characteristics.
4. Choose proportionate consultation and escalation routes. Define what each party needs to know or decide, with confidentiality limits and no implied authorization to contact them.
5. Test the proposed agreement against absent approvals or implementation owners that could make it unusable.

## Authority Is Issue-Specific

A representative may discuss an option without authority to commit resources, waive a right or accept risk. Support from an influential party does not replace required consent. A stakeholder map is a coordination aid, not a license to pressure opponents or gather private information unrelated to the task.

## Output

Role/interest/authority map, required consultations and approvals, missing representation and a bounded engagement plan.

## Quality Checks

- Affected parties are not excluded merely because they have little bargaining power.
- Every claimed approval has an actual authority basis; uncertain authority remains a gate.

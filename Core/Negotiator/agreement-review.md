# Agreement Review

Context: [Negotiator](README.md).

## Purpose

Check term consistency, open items and closure/approval conditions.

## When to Use

Proposed terms need a non-legal operational consistency and closure review.

## Boundary

Not legal certification or signing authority.

## Inputs

Actual draft/version, negotiation record, parties/authority, agreed objectives, constraints and unresolved terms.

## Method

1. Compare the draft with the actual accepted package. Identify additions, omissions, conflicting versions and conditional terms that have not been satisfied.
2. Map obligations to actor, deliverable, timing, acceptance, dependencies and evidence. Check that one party’s duty does not assume an uncommitted action by another.
3. Walk through normal execution, delay, partial performance, disagreement, change and termination. Flag ambiguities and missing ownership without inventing legal remedies.
4. Check total consequences against reservation limits and approval scope. Distinguish discussed, proposed, accepted in principle and formally approved/signed status.
5. Return exact clarification questions and the gate for closure. Seek qualified domain/legal review where enforceability, regulated obligations or material rights are involved.

## Operational Closure

A friendly message or persuasive summary is not an enforceable or executable agreement. Confirm how acceptance will be evidenced under the actual process; do not prescribe a universal legal form. If an important term is unresolved, preserve its open status rather than smoothing it into “mutual understanding.” After closure, retain implementation owners and a review path.

## Output

Term/obligation matrix, discrepancies, open questions, qualified-review needs, approval status and implementation handoff.

## Quality Checks

- No commitment is attributed to a party who has not actually accepted it within authority.
- The review separates operational clarity from legal validity and does not claim certification.

## Handoffs

Domain specialists assess legal, commercial or other professional effects; [negotiation](negotiation.md) handles unresolved exchange and the actual owner approves closure.

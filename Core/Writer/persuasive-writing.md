# Persuasive Writing

Context: [Writer](README.md).

## Purpose

Present an honest case for a voluntary, informed action.

## When to Use

An honest case must help a reader evaluate a proposed voluntary action.

## Boundary

Do not conceal downside, fabricate social proof or exploit vulnerability.

## Inputs

Audience task, proposed action, supported value claim, evidence, material downside, alternatives and communication mandate.

## Method

1. State the action and why it could serve the reader’s legitimate interests. Do not assume the sender’s benefit is the reader’s benefit.
2. Build the case from relevant evidence and a clear mechanism. Separate demonstrated benefit from forecast, aspiration or testimonial.
3. Address the strongest material objection and disclose costs, risks, conditions and credible alternatives needed for informed choice.
4. Choose proportionate tone and an accessible next step. Avoid fabricated urgency, fake social proof, disguised sponsorship or exploiting vulnerability.
5. Check the draft against actual capabilities and authority. A compelling promise must not exceed what can be delivered or approved; sending remains a separate authorized action.

## Persuasion Through Informed Choice

Strength comes from relevance, clarity and credible support, not hiding the alternative. Acknowledge when no action or another option may be better under different preferences. Do not change statistical framing, omit a denominator or smooth away uncertainty to increase conversion. Respect refusal and do not design pressure loops around a vulnerable reader.

## Output

Persuasive draft with supported benefit, honest objection response, material conditions and a clear voluntary next step.

## Quality Checks

- The reader could make an informed choice from the stated benefits and material limitations.
- No fictional facts, commitments, scarcity or endorsement are used to strengthen the argument.

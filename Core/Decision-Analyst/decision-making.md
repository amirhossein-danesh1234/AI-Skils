# Decision Making

Context: [Decision-Analyst](README.md).

## Purpose

Frame the due choice, compare alternatives and decide how much evidence to buy.

## When to Use

A real choice is due under competing objectives, uncertainty or irreversible exposure.

## Boundary

Recommendations are not owner approval or specialist policy.

## Inputs

Decision owner, choice and deadline, objectives, constraints, feasible alternatives, relevant evidence and consequences of delay.

## Method

1. Frame the smallest decision that must be made now, including no action, defer, narrow or staged commitment where feasible. Separate choosing an objective from choosing how to achieve an accepted one.
2. Screen alternatives against hard constraints supplied by the owner and specialists. Compare remaining outcomes on a common horizon, including opportunity cost, switching/exit costs and displaced obligations.
3. Distinguish reversible experiments from irreversible commitments by actual recovery time, cost and residual effects. Break a large decision into smaller gates only if the early step preserves meaningful options.
4. Test the preferred option against uncertain inputs and owner preferences. Seek a decision-flipping observation rather than collecting facts that cannot change the choice.
5. Recommend act, stage, defer or decline with reasons, downside, owner decision and a reconsideration trigger. A recommendation can be complete even when it rejects the proposed action.

## Information Versus Delay

With defensible probabilities and utilities, the expected value of information is the improvement from choosing after the evidence rather than now; compare it with research cost and lost value from delay. Without that basis, ask qualitatively: could a plausible result change the option, can it arrive before the decision, and is the avoidable downside worth its cost? Low-value research refines an irrelevant estimate; high-value research resolves a binding, decision-sensitive uncertainty. Perfect information is rarely available and waiting is itself a choice.

## Output

Decision brief with options, recommendation, trade-offs, information/deferral choice, authorized next commitment and revisit conditions.

## Quality Checks

- The recommendation does not depend on invented preferences, probabilities or unavailable options.
- Staging does not disguise an irreversible first step; the no-action consequence is represented fairly.

## Handoffs

Use [option-comparison](option-comparison.md) for alternatives and [Planner](../Planner/README.md) only after a course is accepted. Domain specialists supply substantive constraints.
